The strategy

One screen — never lose the plot
Route
EB-2 as an Advanced Degree Professional (Harvard master's — eligibility settled, nothing more to prove), with a National Interest Waiver so you self-petition without an employer.
Endeavor
AI and quantitative methods applied to improving education access and outcomes. Education is the headline; finance and research are supporting evidence — never co-equal storylines.
Advantages
  • Bangladesh birth → current priority date → concurrent I-485 → EAD without the H-1B lottery
  • OPT now + STEM extension available (LDIT is STEM-eligible) → runway to ~mid-2028
  • LDIT degree matches the endeavor cleanly
The test
All three Dhanasar prongs, each passing independently — proven by you, by preponderance of the evidence.
The climate
Aggregate NIW approvals run low (~⅓) under stricter standards; your sub-profile — advanced STEM-linked degree, AI, publications — historically fares better, but only the evidence file makes that true.

Phase II

Production

3

The endeavor statement

Write FIRST — the brief, letters, and CV all hang off it
How to execute
  1. Block 90 minutes. Write one rough paragraph answering four questions: what do my tools do · for whom · what broken thing in education do they fix · what will exist by 2029.
  2. Send the rough paragraph to Claude → shape into three sections: the problem (national scale, with statistics), my work (specific tools, methods, pilot results), the 3-year plan (one milestone block per year).
  3. Concreteness pass: every sentence gets a name, number, or institution. "Improve learning" → "raise reading outcomes measured by X for community-college students."
  4. Trap pass: delete any sentence about banking prestige or the general importance of education. Both fail Prong 1.
  5. Freeze v1. Revisit only after letters are drafted — recommenders' language often sharpens it.

Evidence by prong

Prong 2 is the battleground
Prong 1 — substantial merit & national importance
Prong 2 — well-positioned (the battleground)
Prong 3 — beneficial to waive the job offer
How to execute
  1. Prong 1 (one afternoon): download and print the NSTC Critical & Emerging Technologies list and the Dept. of Education strategic plan; highlight the lines your endeavor serves; save to 04-Evidence with citations.
  2. This week (30 min): create Google Scholar + ORCID profiles; link your two publications; screenshot the profiles, dated.
  3. Standing routine: first of every month, export tool analytics to PDF → 04-Evidence. Quarterly, screenshot citation counts.
  4. July: draft a conference abstract from the pilot data; target education-research or EdTech venues with fall deadlines.
  5. August: register as a peer reviewer with 2–3 ed-tech / education journals (most have an open reviewer signup).
  6. Prong 3: write the waiver narrative as one page AFTER the endeavor statement exists — it reuses the same facts pointed at "why no single employer fits."

Letters of recommendation

~5–8 total — objective evidence carries the case; letters interpret it
Stack A — independent expert letters (3–5)
Stack B — collaborator letters (2–3)
Stretch
Process
How to execute
  1. Build the list from five pools: your papers' co-authors' wider networks · researchers you cite who'd recognize the work · HGSE / education faculty you did NOT work under · EdTech founders or product leaders · education-policy researchers. 10–12 names into the matrix.
  2. Outreach email, six lines: who you are (one line) · what the work is (one line + link to pilot write-up) · the ask: "a letter supporting my immigration petition — I'll provide a full draft for your edits" · the deadline · gratitude. Send in batches of four.
  3. For each yes: within 7 days, send a one-page work summary + a complete draft letter tailored to that person's vantage point. Different letters must not share paragraphs — adjudicators notice template letters.
  4. Each draft cites specific exhibits ("the pilot study found X") so every claim is corroborated.
  5. Follow up at 2 weeks, once. Target: 4 signed by October 31 — that's the gate condition.

11

Rules & warnings

Read monthly — they govern every step
  1. Eligibility is SETTLED. No licenses, certifications, or EB-1 criteria needed — ever. Stop shopping eligibility.
  2. All three prongs must each pass. No averaging. One endeavor, plural evidence.
  3. The burden of proof is yours, by preponderance of the evidence.
  4. Letters are claims; exhibits are evidence. Objective evidence carries the case; letters interpret it.
  5. EAD approvals ≠ petition strength. The petition is built, not assumed.
  6. Credentials are threshold items, not arguments. The case is impact documentation.
  7. Never embellish. Misrepresentation = permanent bars.
  8. Consistency across every document. Mismatches trigger RFEs.
  9. 20 home-campus users = a seed, not adoption. Outside reach is the conversion.
  10. Finance is supporting cast, never the endeavor.
  11. Procedure is unforgiving. Current editions, exact fees, right lockbox, certified translations.
  12. Institutionalize doubt. Clinic, limited-scope review, red-team.
  13. Keep USCIS and SEVP current. AR-11 within 10 days of moving; SEVP within 10 days of job changes.
